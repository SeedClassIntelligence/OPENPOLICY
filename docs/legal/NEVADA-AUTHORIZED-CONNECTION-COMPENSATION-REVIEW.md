## Compliance question

Evaluate whether Open Policy may charge a licensed provider the V2
`AUTHORIZED_CONNECTION` fee after consumer selection, express authorization,
controlled disclosure and successful connection without Open Policy itself
holding a Nevada producer license, given the implemented conduct and neutrality
controls below.

This is a factual issue-spotting record, not legal advice. Nevada has not been
shown to require a special referral-fee permit or case-specific government
approval. Runtime charging remains off until separate operational activation;
the durable compliance record exists to enforce the statutory conduct boundary,
not to manufacture another regulatory approval process.

## Official Nevada authority

The current official Nevada Legislature text defines “negotiate,” “sell,” and
“solicit” in NRS 683A.065, .072 and .074; requires a producer license for those
activities in NRS 683A.201; and controls payment or receipt of valuable
consideration in NRS 683A.361. Subsection 4 permits payment to a person who does
not sell, solicit or negotiate unless NRS 686A.110 or .120 is violated.

- [NRS Chapter 683A — Nevada Legislature](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/Division/Legal/LawLibrary/nrs/NRS-683A.html)

The DOI memorandum explains that classification depends on the particulars of
the referral program. The fee's name, timing or flat amount therefore cannot
decide legality by itself; implemented conduct must be evaluated.

## Implemented product facts

1. Consumers enroll without charge and upload an existing policy.
2. Open Policy extracts evidence and requires consumer review, correction and
   verification of the baseline.
3. Consumers do not set target terms or direct providers to meet a price.
4. Authorized providers independently determine the coverage, price and terms
   they submit.
5. Open Policy has no minimum price-improvement gate and preserves every valid
   documented offer for factual review.
6. Comparisons show factual price and coverage differences. The platform does
   not rank providers, recommend an offer, declare a winner or prompt providers
   to alter terms.
7. The consumer alone selects an offer, retains current coverage, requests an
   additional cohort while the original window is open, or closes the process.
8. Providers cannot see competitors' offers, consumer decline reasons or
   platform pricing signals.
9. Post-selection application, underwriting, revised terms, binding and
   issuance occur through the licensed provider's own channel.
10. Availability, viewing, engagement, offer submission, selection, binding and
    verified outcome are $0 events under V2.

## V2 compensation event

`AUTHORIZED_CONNECTION` is the sole billable marketplace transaction event.
It may become charge-eligible only when the consumer selected the provider,
granted the required authorization, controlled disclosure completed, the
connection succeeded, an applicable current agreement exists, the line is
operational, an approved compensation determination permits the charge, and
idempotency proves it has not already been charged.

The event is distinct from selection and from a bound-policy outcome. Open
Policy receives no separate V2 marketplace fee for binding.

## Conduct boundaries to confirm and preserve

1. Does Open Policy “obtain insurance from insurers or purchasers” within NRS
   683A.065 when it facilitates disclosure and connection but does not apply or
   bind on the consumer's behalf?
2. Which comparison labels and plain-language explanations remain factual rather
   than advice about substantive terms?
3. Does any public or in-product call to action ask or urge a person to apply for
   a particular insurance kind from a particular insurer under NRS 683A.074?
4. Is the provider-paid line-specific connection fee permissible under NRS
   683A.361(4) with these neutrality and handoff boundaries?
5. Do NRS 686A.110 or .120 prohibit or condition the fee, consumer experience,
   provider promotion or incentive structure?
6. Must Open Policy, an affiliate or any personnel hold another Nevada license?
7. What disclosure, advertising, supervision and record-retention conditions
   should apply?
